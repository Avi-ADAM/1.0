## 0. The rules (read before sending anything)

1. **Public, respectful, one at a time.** Reply where they posted, or use the contact path
   their own website offers. No scraped emails, no enrichment tools, no sequences. The
   scan's strongest pattern (10/10 prospects) is that the right channel is the forum thread
   itself.
2. **One question, not a pitch.** Every message ends with a question about *their* workflow.
   The product comes up only after they answered (days 3–5, see §4).
3. **Design partner, not customer.** What we offer is a hand-held first pool — we set it
   up, we watch it, we fix what breaks — in exchange for the numbers and the story.
   That is the whole offer. Three true stories are worth more than a thousand words of copy.
4. **Revalidate stale signals** before contact: Matturdayyyy (2024-11), Max (2024-03),
   Laamel (2024-08). Lead with "is this still live?".
5. **Never imply legal or financial advice.** Co-op formation questions stay about
   workflow — who decides, who does, how money is shared *by agreement*.
6. **Honest state of the product.** It is live, it is Hebrew-first with full English, the
   consensus engine is open source, and we are small. Say so.

## 1. The offer, in one paragraph

> We are looking for three groups to run one real pool with, hand-held. A pool is a page
> where people who want the same thing — a weekly farm box, a workshop that only runs
> with eight, a bulk order for the neighbourhood — sign individually, and the thing
> happens only when enough have signed. No group chat, no committee, nobody pays before
> the threshold. We set the page up with you, we stay on it until it activates, and we
> ask for two things back: your numbers, and permission to tell the story.

Vocabulary when talking outside: **Pool** (maagad), **Threshold Offer**, **Wish** (ratson),
**Weave** (rikma). Internal names stay in the code.

The hook that journalists and forum readers remember: **"we removed the group from group
buying."**

## 4. After they answer

**Days 3–5 — map, don't demo.** One call, 20 minutes, five questions:

1. Walk me through the last time you did this (season signup / class / bulk order). Where
   did it start, where did it end?
2. How many people did you need, how many did you get, how long did it take?
3. What tools were involved? (Expect: spreadsheet + WhatsApp/Facebook + bank transfers.)
4. What happens when someone drops out after committing?
5. If the page did the chasing for you, what would you do with the hours?

Write the answers down as one workflow — *decision → commitment → money* — before showing
anything. Only then: a two-minute walkthrough configured to **their** pool (their product,
their threshold, their deadline, their currency). Never the generic demo.

**The pilot setup (what "hand-held" means, concretely):**

- [ ] Their pool page exists (`/maagad/<id>`), named in their words, in their language.
- [ ] One threshold offer: price, minimum, maximum, deadline, cancellation terms in plain
      language.
- [ ] The share card renders (`/api/og/maagad/<id>.png`) and the link unfurls in WhatsApp
      and Telegram — test it yourself before they share it.
- [ ] They have the ready-made share text (the page gives it to every signer).
- [ ] A named person on our side watching the pool daily until it activates or expires.
- [ ] A date for the write-up call, agreed up front.

**Success in seven days (from the scan):** three conversations, one written workflow map,
one group willing to run a real pool with real signatures.

## 5. What we give back

- The numbers, in their words: *needed / got / days / dropouts / hours saved*.
- A case page they can link to from their own site.
- Priority on what breaks for them — they are shaping the product, and we say so publicly.
